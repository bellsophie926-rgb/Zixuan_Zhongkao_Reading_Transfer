A Small Repair Table at School

A broken thing does not always need to be thrown away.

At the start of this term, I thought my schoolbag was finished. One zipper was broken, and one strap was coming loose. I planned to ask my parents for a new one. Then I noticed a small sign beside the library: REPAIR TABLE — Wednesdays at lunch. Ms. Zhou, a technology teacher, and four students had started it to keep useful things out of the rubbish bin.

Students can bring bags, coats or pencil cases. The volunteers keep needles, thread, buttons, spare zips and cloth patches in labelled boxes. The table is not a shop, and they do not promise to repair everything. If a bag is badly torn, they explain why it may not be safe to use. For small problems, they show students how to sew on a button or cover a hole with a patch.

When I brought my bag, I only said, “Can you fix this?” One volunteer gave me a blank look. Ms. Zhou asked me to point to the broken zipper and explain when it had started to stick. The volunteer then showed me how to move it gently and check whether the teeth were bent. We found that the zipper itself was fine; a loose thread was blocking it.

I spent ten minutes removing the thread while the volunteer watched. The bag was not perfect, but it worked again. More important, I learned how to check a zipper before it gets stuck. The table now keeps a short record of each repair, so the team can see which problems happen most often. They hope to teach more students these small skills next term.
